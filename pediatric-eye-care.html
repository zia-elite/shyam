Pediatric Eye Care

Healthy visual development depends on timely screening and follow-up.

Children’s eye care requires age-appropriate examination and early attention to visual development. Gupta Eye Center provides assessment for concerns such as refractive error, amblyopia, strabismus and other childhood eye problems, with clear guidance for parents on treatment and follow-up.

When to seek evaluation

When an eye assessment may be useful.

  • A failed vision screening or difficulty seeing the board/reading
  • An eye that turns inward, outward, upward or downward
  • Frequent squinting, head tilting, closing one eye or sitting unusually close
  • Eye rubbing, headaches or visual complaints
  • A family history or medical condition that increases risk of childhood eye disease
Clinical evaluation

What may be checked during assessment.

  • Age-appropriate visual-acuity assessment
  • Refraction to check for glasses power
  • Eye alignment and movement testing
  • Pupil, front-of-eye and dilated retinal examination when indicated
  • Additional testing based on the child’s age, cooperation and suspected condition
Treatment pathways

Treatment is matched to the diagnosis and the eye.

The options below are common approaches that may be discussed. The appropriate plan depends on examination findings, diagnosis, eye health and individual needs.

01

Glasses

Correcting refractive error can improve clarity and is often a key part of treating or preventing amblyopia.

02

Amblyopia treatment

Patching or other treatments may be used to encourage the weaker eye and brain to develop better vision.

03

Strabismus and other care

Depending on the condition, treatment can include glasses, observation, exercises for selected problems, medicines or surgery.

Your consultation

What to expect at your consultation.

01

History & symptoms

Your vision, symptoms, previous eye care, medicines, health conditions and treatment goals are reviewed first.

02

Eye examination

Tests are selected according to the concern and may include vision testing, eye pressure, slit-lamp examination, dilation or imaging.

03

Plan & follow-up

Findings are reviewed with you, along with appropriate options, expected benefits, limitations, risks and follow-up.

Pediatric Eye Care FAQ

Common questions about children’s eye care.

These answers provide general guidance. Diagnosis and treatment decisions require an individual eye examination.

Book Consultation

A child can have reduced vision in one eye without realizing it. Screening helps detect amblyopia risk factors, refractive errors and eye misalignment early, when treatment can be more effective.

Amblyopia, often called lazy eye, is reduced vision caused by abnormal visual development. It can be associated with eye misalignment, unequal refractive error between the eyes, or anything that blocks a clear image during childhood.

Strabismus is misalignment of the eyes. One eye may turn inward, outward, upward or downward. It can affect binocular vision and may contribute to amblyopia.

A child should be referred for a comprehensive eye evaluation if they fail screening, cannot complete testing, have symptoms or abnormal visual behaviour, or have risk factors for eye disease.

Some prescriptions change as the eyes grow, but whether glasses can be reduced or stopped depends on the type and amount of refractive error and the child’s visual development. Follow-up determines this.

Persistent eye turning, head tilting, closing one eye, unusually close viewing, a white pupil reflex, frequent headaches/eye strain or concerns raised by school screening should prompt assessment.
